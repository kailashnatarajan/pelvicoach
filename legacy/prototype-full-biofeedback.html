How to read these charts. The x-axis is time in seconds. The y-axis is signal amplitude in arbitrary units (the file does not state a physical unit). All three charts share the same time axis, so a moment lines up vertically across them.

1. Raw contaminated EMG

The simulated electrode signal after the simulator added artifacts (listed above, for example baseline drift and 50 Hz powerline interference). It shows what an unfiltered sensor could deliver. Interference and drift sit on top of the muscle activity, so amplitude alone is not a reliable cue.

2. Processed EMG

The same signal after the simulator's filtering step (Colab notebook, or the in-browser generator for random cases). Artifacts are reduced, so the bursts of activity during the shaded contraction intervals stand out more clearly.

3. Extracted activity envelope

A smooth curve of how strong the signal is over time, computed by the simulator from the processed EMG. It rises while the simulated muscle is active. The dashed line is the illustrative level: an adjustable demo threshold (see rule settings), not a clinical criterion.

Illustrative rule settings

Adjustable demo thresholds for signal-quality flags and the amplitude level. They are not clinical criteria.
